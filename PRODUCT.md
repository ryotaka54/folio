# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

International VIPs, Tokyo nightlife aficionados, tourists seeking premium experiences, event promoters, and bottle-service clientele. They arrive at this site while planning a night out or looking to reserve a table — they're in a buying mood, not a browsing mood. The site must make them feel that walking through 1 OAK's door is an event in itself.

## Product Purpose

1 OAK Tokyo's online presence. The site sells the *feeling* of exclusivity before the visitor ever steps inside — through atmosphere, imagery, and deliberate restraint. Success means a visitor leaves either with a reservation made or a strong compulsion to make one.

Sections: Hero, Upcoming Events, VIP Reservations, Gallery, About, Footer/Contact.

## Brand Personality

Void-black luxury. A fashion house that happens to be a nightclub. Confrontational restraint — the site doesn't beg for your attention; it withholds it. Three words: **nocturnal · confrontational · precise**.

## Anti-references

- Generic nightclub templates (spinning vinyl, rainbow flyer energy, DJ headshot grids, cheap neon glow effects on everything)
- Busy over-animated sites where everything bounces simultaneously
- Corporate hospitality (stock photos, system fonts, safe flat-button CTAs)

## Design Principles

1. **Silence commands more than noise.** Black space is not empty — it's the product. Don't fill it.
2. **Fashion house, not entertainment venue.** Typography and spacing do more than imagery. The club is one of the world's most exclusive; the site must feel as selective as the door.
3. **Photography is evidence, not decoration.** Each image is chosen to close the sale, not to populate a grid.
4. **One idea per viewport.** Scrolling reveals the next world. No section competes with another.
5. **Motion earns its place.** One orchestrated page-load sequence beats scattered scroll-triggered fades on every section.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Reduced-motion alternative for all animations (typically crossfade or instant transition). All form inputs properly labeled. Color contrast: white-on-black easily passes; confirm any tinted or muted text variants.
